Lessons

Each lesson has the same five parts: the idea, a visualization to play with, a walkthrough of real Kindling code, an exercise with hints and a hidden solution, and a "you now know" checklist.

New to games? Do the 2D track in order, then the design track before your first jam. The 3D track assumes you have done 2D lessons 1 to 4.

2D track

Canvas 2D games from the loop up: input, collision, physics, levels and juice.

  1. The game loop Why games update in fixed steps, and how the accumulator makes a game run the same on every screen. About 20 minutes.
  2. Drawing and input Draw with Canvas 2D, and read keyboard, mouse, touch and gamepad through named actions. About 25 minutes.
  3. Collision Detect when boxes and circles touch, and push them apart with the smallest move. About 30 minutes.
  4. Platformer physics Gravity, velocity, moving one axis at a time, and the tricks that make jumping feel good. About 35 minutes.
  5. Tilemaps and the camera Build levels from text grids and scroll them with a smooth camera that stays inside the level. About 30 minutes.
  6. Juice: particles, shake, tweens and sound Make actions feel good with particles, screen shake, tweens, hit-stop and sound effects. About 30 minutes.

3D track

Matrices, meshes and light with WebGL 2, a raycaster with no WebGL at all, and a first-person walk.

  1. 3D transforms: model, view, projection How three matrices move a 3D object to the right place on a 2D screen. About 35 minutes.
  2. Meshes and lighting Triangles, normals and Phong lighting: ambient, diffuse and specular. About 35 minutes.
  3. The raycaster: 3D without WebGL Cast one ray per screen column across a grid map and draw walls whose height is one over the distance. About 30 minutes.
  4. A first-person walk Mouse look with pointer lock, walking relative to where you look, collision with 3D boxes, and fog. About 35 minutes.

Game design track

Scope a jam game you can finish, make it feel good, playtest it and ship it.

  1. Scope: a game you can finish Pick a jam idea you can finish: one verb, a must/should/could list, and a budget that assumes everything takes twice as long. About 20 minutes.
  2. Game feel Why the same game can feel stiff or great: response, easing, anticipation, and squash and stretch. About 25 minutes.
  3. Playtest and ship Watch someone play without helping, write down what happens, fix the biggest problem, and ship to itch.io. About 25 minutes.
