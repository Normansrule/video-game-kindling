Scope: a game you can finish

Game design track · lesson 1 of 3 · about 20 minutes

Most jam games that fail are not bad ideas. They are good ideas that were three times too big.

1 The idea

A jam game needs one verb (jump, shoot, push, match, dodge) done well, one way to win, one way to lose, and sound. Everything else is a bonus. Write your idea as a sentence: "You verb to goal, but problem." For example: "You jump between rooftops to deliver mail, but the city is sinking."

Then sort features into must (the game does not exist without it), should and could. Build every must before any should. Aim for a playable, ugly version (an MVP, minimum viable product) by the end of the first quarter of the jam.

Budget honestly: you will work about 40% of the jam's hours, and everything takes about twice as long as you think.

2 See it

Tick features and pick a jam length. The bar turns red when the plan cannot fit.

Features (your honest guess in hours):

    Real working time = jam length × 0.4 (sleep, food, life, setup, submitting). Real cost = your guess × 2, because everything takes twice as long in a jam.

    3 Walkthrough

    1. Hour 0 to 1: read the theme, write five one-sentence pitches, pick the one whose verb you can build tonight. Write it at the top of your notes.
    2. Hour 1: make the game folder with ./jam/new-game.sh my-game (see jam/). It already runs, so you start from a working game, not a blank page.
    3. First 25% of the jam: the verb, one level, win and lose. Gray boxes are fine. If it is not fun with boxes, art will not save it; change the idea now, while it is cheap.
    4. Middle 50%: shoulds, then juice (sound, particles, shake). Playtest at least twice (next lessons).
    5. Last 25%: no new features. Fix, polish, write the page, and submit two hours early. Uploads fail and pages need screenshots. The jam checklist and scope cutter walk through each hour.

    4 Exercise

    The theme is "Only one". Write a one-sentence pitch in the verb/goal/problem form, then a must/should/could list with an hour guess for each item. Use the budget above with a 48-hour jam until the bar is green.

    Hint 1

    "Only one" can be one button, one life, one bullet, one room, one minute, one move per turn...

    Hint 2

    If your musts alone turn the bar orange, the idea is too big. Remove a whole system (inventory, dialogue, multiplayer), not just a level.

    Solution

    An example answer:

    Pitch: You fire your ONLY bullet and steer it through a room to hit every target,
       but if it stops moving, you lose.

Must (13 h guessed, 26 h real):
  - bullet steers toward the mouse, constant speed          3 h
  - walls bounce it, targets break on hit                     3 h
  - 5 small rooms as tilemaps                                 4 h
  - win and lose screens, restart with R                      1 h
  - sounds: fire, bounce, hit, win, lose                      1 h
  - itch.io page and upload                                   1 h
Should: particles and shake on hits (1 h), a timer and best time (1 h)
Could:  moving targets, a level select, music

    Tick each one you could explain to a friend. Your ticks are saved in this browser only.
